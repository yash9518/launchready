An AI-powered checker that reviews packaging artwork against a rulebook and the approved product data. It says what is definitely wrong, what needs a person, and what passed. Every finding has its rule quote and its evidence on the artwork.

AI reads · code checks · people decide

1. The problem

Before final artwork goes to print, someone must confirm that every required element is present and correctly placed, that the text and numbers match the approved content sheet, that claims are true for the recipe, and that the nutrition panel adds up. Today this means proofreading a PDF, a spreadsheet and a rulebook by hand. The costly errors are cross-document: a claim contradicted by an ingredient, a value that differs from the content sheet, an allergen missing from the "Contains" line. Reprints and recalls are expensive. A checker that raises false alarms gets ignored, so avoiding false alarms matters as much as catching errors.

2. Our approach

  1. Read the artwork exactly. From a print PDF's text layer we take the real words, fonts, sizes and positions, so there is no OCR guessing. We find the dieline panels and the principal display panel, measure character heights in mm, decode barcodes, and detect text painted over by other artwork. Photos and scans go through neural OCR with a second reading of uncertain words.
  2. Turn text into facts with sources. Identity, net quantity, ingredients, allergens, Nutrition Facts / nutrition table, claims, address and barcode. Each fact keeps the IDs of the lines it came from.
  3. Turn the rulebook into rule cards. Each card has a verbatim quote, a source, a severity, "why it matters", an optional effective date, and one of 24 deterministic check types. Free-text regulations are compiled by an LLM, and every quote must be found in the source.
  4. Check with code, verify before alarming. Presence and placement, word-for-word copy, nutrition arithmetic and %DV, claims against nutrients and ingredients, allergen coverage, GS1 check digits and text height. Anything reported "missing" gets a second look first.
  5. Decide with an explicit policy. AI alone can never fail a rule.

How AI output is kept honest. The rule compiler may only choose from the fixed check catalogue, and each quote must be found in the source or the card is dropped. The second reader must cite OCR line IDs; disagreement sends the item to review. Judgment rules can pass or be flagged for review, never fail. Follow-up answers use only the finding's rule quote and evidence. Every AI action is logged in the UI as accepted, rejected or downgraded, and responses are cached so results can be reproduced. Without an API key every deterministic check still runs, and judgment rules go to a person.

5. Result on Esko's sample carton

Dancing Fish Granola US carton (DeskPack PDF) + Esko's US content sheet (Excel) + a 31-rule US rulebook (FDA 21 CFR 101, FALCPA and USDA organic rules, plus company standards). Not ready to print: 18 must fix, 4 need a person, 7 passed, 2 coming, in about 10 seconds.

  • "Suitable for Vegans" and a Certified Vegan mark, but honey in the ingredients. "No added sugar", but honey, coconut sugar and cane sugar.
  • Almonds, pecans and walnuts in the ingredients, but no "Contains:" statement, only "may contain" advisory text.
  • 166 kcal declared vs about 535 kcal from the macros; 10 wrong %DVs; carbohydrate 66 g on the label vs 15 g in the content sheet.
  • Net weight only on the back panel, with no "Net Wt" term and no oz. Organic claim without a certifier. Address without city, state or ZIP.
  • 19 lines of an old nutrition table hidden under the artwork in the print file (sent to review).
  • The content sheet's own errors: its sugars exceed its carbohydrate, and its calories don't match its own macros.

7. What makes it trustworthy

  • Evidence: every finding draws a box on the artwork and quotes its rule, source and "why it matters".
  • Uncertainty: reading confidence on every finding; review instead of guessing.
  • Verify before alarm: near-miss search and re-scans before anything is reported missing.
  • Traceability: "What was read" and "How it was checked" show every fact's source and every AI action.
  • Effective dates: check against the rules in force on any date; proposed rules are shown, not enforced.
  • Source data checked too: contradictions inside the content sheet are reported.

8. Run it

Python 3.10+. run.sh (macOS / Linux) or run.bat (Windows) creates a virtual environment and starts the app at http://localhost:8000. Optional AI: copy .env.example to .env, add GEMINI_API_KEY, and check it with python tools/check_ai.py. Tests: python -m pytest -q. Evaluation: python tools/evaluate.py.

Limitations: the rulebooks are illustrative interpretations written for this demo, not legal advice. Fact extractors cover food labels (US, India); other categories need new extractors on the same engine.
